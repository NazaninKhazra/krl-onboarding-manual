# 15. Supervisor Guide

This guide is for faculty and graduate students who supervise research trainees in K.R.L. or in a program built on the same model, such as the Vic Research Hub. It covers how to post a project, set up an appointment, give feedback, and close it.

Supervisors who are new to research mentoring should also read the Research Mentoring TA Manual (<https://github.com/NazaninKhazra/research-mentoring-ta-manual>). It was written for teaching assistants in research-based courses, and its chapters on giving feedback apply equally to lab supervision. Chapters 2 (the TA as research mentor), 9 (feedback calibration), and 12 (weekly reports) are the most relevant.

## Why group mentoring

The National Academies' 2019 report on mentorship in STEMM lists group or collective mentoring, mentoring triads, and mentoring networks among effective mentorship structures. It also encourages students to build a "constellation" of mentors. K.R.L. is built this way. Members learn from the supervisor, from graduate assistants, and from each other in weekly group meetings. Graduate assistants act as near-peer mentors and are themselves mentored by the faculty supervisor.

## Posting a project

A project posting tells students what the work is and what it requires. Each posting includes:

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Title and short description (100 to 150 words) | Measuring the effect of a 2024 rent-control change on tenant turnover |
| Time window | Six weeks, October to mid-November |
| Hours per week | 5 to 8 |
| Supervision | One 30-minute meeting per week |
| Skills needed | Basic Python or R; no prior research experience needed |
| Deliverable | A cleaned panel dataset with a codebook |

Specific postings attract more applicants than open invitations to "help with research," and they make the selection fairer, because every applicant sees the same description.

## Choosing a scope

Match the task to the time available.

| Length | Suitable tasks |
|---|---|
| 2 to 4 weeks | Data collection, cleaning one dataset, a short literature review, replicating one table |
| 1 to 3 months | Building a merged dataset, a full literature review, a first descriptive analysis |
| A term or a year | A section of a working paper, a full empirical analysis, a seminar talk |

Short appointments work well as a first step. A trainee who does good work can continue under a new project description.

## Starting an appointment

1. Arrange the University Student Research Trainee letter with your department office and complete the project description with the trainee ([Document 2](02-trainee-contract-template.md)). Agree on the deliverable and what the closing letter will describe.
2. Share the first-week checklist ([Document 3](03-first-week-checklist.md)) and set up data access.
3. Assign a short practice task, then a first real task the trainee can finish in a week. Early success builds confidence and shows you how the trainee works.

## Giving feedback

- Reply to each weekly report within a week, in a few sentences. Say whether the work is on track, name any problem, and set one priority for the next week.
- Comment on the reasoning as well as the output. Ask the trainee to explain a choice before you correct it.
- Point to the step that went wrong and let the trainee fix it.
- Praise specific work: "The codebook made the merge easy to check" is more useful than "Good job."
- If two weekly reports show no progress, meet the trainee within the week. A stalled report trail often means the trainee is stuck and has not said so.

## Inclusive supervision

In economics, women report lower confidence than men at comparable levels of achievement (Sarsons and Xu, 2021). Students who are new to research or are the first in their family at university may also hesitate to speak up. In practice:

- Give challenging tasks to quiet members as readily as to confident ones.
- Explain the unwritten rules of research, such as how to ask a question in a seminar or how to email a professor.
- Read hesitation as unfamiliarity with research norms. It is rarely a sign of low ability.
- Offer a short one-on-one, such as a walk-and-talk, to members who find formal meetings intimidating.

[Document 16](16-wellbeing-and-belonging.md) describes how the lab supports members' wellbeing.

## Your own time

A realistic commitment is about one hour per trainee per week, including meetings and feedback. Group meetings and team projects reduce this, because trainees help one another. Do not take on more trainees than you can meet regularly.

## Closing an appointment

Use the closing packet ([Document 13](13-closing-packet.md)). Write the closing letter within two weeks of receiving the deliverable. Describe what the trainee did with specific evidence. A letter that names tasks and outputs tells a reader more than general praise.
