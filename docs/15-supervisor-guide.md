# 15. Supervisor Guide

This guide is for faculty and graduate students who supervise research trainees in K.R.L. or in a program built on the same model, such as the Vic Research Hub. It covers how to post a project, set up an appointment, give feedback, and close it.

## Posting a project

A project posting tells students what the work is and what it requires. Each posting includes:

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Title and short description (100 to 150 words) | Measuring the effect of a 2024 rent-control change on tenant turnover |
| Time window | Six weeks, October to mid-November |
| Hours per week | 5 to 8 |
| Supervision | One 30-minute meeting per week |
| Skills needed | Basic Python or R; no prior research experience needed |
| Deliverable | A cleaned panel dataset with a codebook |

Keep the posting specific. Students apply more readily to a defined task than to an open invitation to "help with research."

## Choosing a scope

Match the task to the time available.

| Length | Suitable tasks |
|---|---|
| 2 to 4 weeks | Data collection, cleaning a single dataset, a short literature review, replicating one table |
| 1 to 3 months | Building a merged dataset, a full literature review, a first descriptive analysis |
| A term or a year | A section of a working paper, a full empirical analysis, a seminar presentation |

Short appointments work well as a first step. A trainee who does good work can continue under a new agreement.

## Starting an appointment

1. Complete the trainee agreement ([Document 2](02-trainee-contract-template.md)) with the trainee. Agree on the deliverable and the content of the closing letter.
2. Share the first-week checklist ([Document 3](03-first-week-checklist.md)) and set up data access.
3. Give a first task the trainee can finish in a week. Early success builds confidence and shows you how the trainee works.

## Giving feedback

- Reply to each weekly report within a week, in a few sentences. Say whether the work is on track, name any problem, and set one priority for the next week.
- Comment on the reasoning as well as the output. Ask the trainee to explain a choice before you correct it.
- Point to the step that went wrong and let the trainee fix it.
- Praise specific work: "The codebook made the merge easy to check" is more useful than "Good job."
- If two weekly reports show no progress, meet the trainee in person within the week. A stalled report often means the trainee is stuck and has not said so.

## Inclusive supervision

Students who are new to research, first-generation students, and students from groups underrepresented in economics often report lower confidence at the same level of performance. Give challenging tasks to quiet students as readily as to confident ones. Explain the unwritten rules of research, such as how to ask a question in a seminar or how to email a professor. Hesitation usually reflects unfamiliarity with research norms.

## Your own time

A realistic commitment is about one hour per trainee per week, including meeting time and feedback. Group meetings and team projects reduce this, because trainees help one another. Do not take on more trainees than you can meet regularly.

## Closing an appointment

Use the closing packet ([Document 13](13-closing-packet.md)). Write the closing letter within two weeks of receiving the deliverable. Describe what the trainee did with specific evidence. A letter that lists tasks and outputs is more useful to a reader than general praise.
