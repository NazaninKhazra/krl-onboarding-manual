# 4. Expectations and Conduct

This document sets out what the lab expects of its members and what members can expect from their supervisor. It applies to trainees, course-based researchers, paid assistants, and graduate assistants.

## What the lab expects of members

**Reliability.** Do the work you agreed to do in the time you agreed. If you cannot meet a deadline, say so before the deadline and propose a new date.

**Communication.** Reply to messages from your supervisor or team within two working days. If you will be away for more than a week, for example during exams, tell your supervisor in advance.

**Meetings.** Attend the weekly group meeting and your scheduled supervisor meetings. If you must miss one, send your weekly report anyway and tell the team in the Teams channel.

**Honest reporting.** Report what you actually did, including errors and failed attempts. A result that does not work is useful information. A result that has been hidden or adjusted puts the whole project at risk.

**Asking for help.** If you are stuck on the same problem for more than two or three hours, ask. Post the question in the Teams channel with the exact error message or the part you do not understand. Other members often know the answer.

**Teamwork.** Help other members when you can, and cover for a teammate who is stretched. Credit others' ideas when you present or write.

**Respect.** Treat other members, seminar audiences, and outside partners with courtesy. Disagreement about research is normal and welcome. Personal criticism is not.

## What members can expect from the supervisor

- A clear first task and a named deliverable, written in the project description.
- Feedback on each weekly report, usually within one week.
- Regular meetings at the frequency in the project description.
- Credit for work done, following [Document 11](11-authorship-and-credit.md).
- A closing letter when the deliverable is complete ([Document 13](13-closing-packet.md)).
- Flexibility when a member is unwell or under heavy academic pressure ([Document 16](16-wellbeing-and-belonging.md)).

## Research integrity

Two University of Toronto policies apply to lab work.

- The **Code of Behaviour on Academic Matters** covers course-based work, including ECO295 and ECO431.
- The **Policy on Ethical Conduct in Research** covers all research done under the University's auspices. It treats fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism as research misconduct.

In practice, do not copy text or code without citing the source. Do not invent, alter, or selectively drop data or results. Follow the AI-use rules in [Document 8](08-ai-use-policy.md). If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you do it.

## University policies in the trainee letter

Trainees also agree, in the University letter, to follow the policies on health and safety, information technology use, copyright, inventions, and workplace harassment and violence ([Document 2](02-trainee-contract-template.md)).

## Problems and concerns

If you have a problem with the work, a teammate, or the supervision, raise it first with your supervisor. If you are not comfortable doing so, contact the Department of Economics undergraduate or graduate office.

## Ending an appointment

An appointment ends on the date in the project description or when the deliverable is complete. Either side may end it earlier with one week's notice. Before you leave, complete the exit steps in [Document 13](13-closing-packet.md) so that your work can be used and credited.

An appointment may also end if a member repeatedly misses meetings or reports without notice. The supervisor will raise the concern in writing and meet the member before taking this step.
