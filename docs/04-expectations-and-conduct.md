# 4. Expectations and Conduct

This document sets out what the lab expects of its members and what members can expect from their supervisor. It applies to trainees, paid assistants, and graduate assistants.

## What the lab expects of members

**Reliability.** Do the work you agreed to do in the time you agreed. If you cannot meet a deadline, say so before the deadline and propose a new date.

**Communication.** Reply to messages from your supervisor or team within two working days. If you will be unavailable for more than a week, for example during exams, tell your supervisor in advance.

**Meetings.** Attend the weekly group meeting and your scheduled supervisor meetings. If you must miss one, send your weekly report anyway and tell the team in the Teams channel.

**Honest reporting.** Report what you actually did, including errors and failed attempts. A result that does not work is useful information. A result that has been hidden or adjusted is a serious problem for everyone on the project.

**Asking for help.** If you are stuck for more than two or three hours on the same problem, ask. Post the question in the Teams channel with the exact error message or the part you do not understand. Other members often know the answer.

**Respect.** Treat other members, seminar audiences, and outside partners with courtesy. Disagreement about research is normal and welcome. Personal criticism is not.

## What members can expect from the supervisor

- A clear first task and a named deliverable, written in the trainee agreement.
- Feedback on each weekly report, usually within one week.
- Regular meetings at the frequency in the agreement.
- Credit for work done, following [Document 11](11-authorship-and-credit.md).
- A closing letter when the deliverable is complete ([Document 13](13-closing-packet.md)).

## Academic integrity

The University of Toronto's Code of Behaviour on Academic Matters applies to research work as it does to coursework. Do not copy text or code without citing the source. Do not invent, alter, or selectively drop data or results. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you do it.

## Problems and concerns

If you have a problem with the work, with a teammate, or with the supervision, raise it first with your supervisor. If you are not comfortable doing so, contact the Department of Economics undergraduate or graduate office.

If you are going through a difficult time, tell your supervisor as much as you wish to share. The lab can reduce your hours or pause your appointment. University of Toronto student support services are listed at <https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/>.

## Ending an appointment

An appointment ends on the date in the agreement or when the deliverable is complete. Either side may end it early with one week's notice. Before you leave, complete the exit steps in [Document 13](13-closing-packet.md) so that your work can be used and credited.

An appointment may also end if a member repeatedly misses meetings or reports without notice. The supervisor will raise the concern in writing before taking this step.
