# 11. Authorship and Credit

Shared authorship is part of the K.R.L. model. Students develop projects together, and those who do substantial work become co-authors of the paper. This policy sets out who becomes a co-author, how other contributions are acknowledged, and how decisions about credit are made. The supervisor discusses it with each team at the start of a project, so that expectations are clear before the work begins.

## Co-authorship

The lab's standard follows the four criteria of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE), which many fields use as a reference point. A member is offered co-authorship when they:

1. made a substantial contribution to the research design, or to the collection, analysis, or interpretation of the data;
2. drafted part of the paper or revised it critically;
3. approved the final version; and
4. agreed to be accountable for the work, including the ability to explain and answer for the part they did.

The ICMJE also says the criteria should not be used to exclude someone who meets the first criterion by denying them the chance to meet the second and third. In K.R.L., every member who makes a substantial contribution is invited to take part in drafting and revising.

Time spent does not by itself qualify a member for authorship. Two weeks of routine data entry, for example, is acknowledged but does not meet the standard. A member who builds the main dataset or writes a section of the paper usually does.

## Acknowledgement

Members who contribute but do not meet the authorship standard are named in the acknowledgements, with a short description of their role (for example, "We thank A. Student for research assistance with data collection").

## Recording contributions

Each paper keeps a contribution record in `docs/contributions.md`, using the CRediT categories (<https://credit.niso.org/>): conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, investigation, methodology, software, visualization, writing (original draft), and writing (review and editing). Members update their own entries, and the supervisor reviews the record at each draft stage.

Weekly reports are the main evidence for this record.

## Author order

In economics, authors are usually listed in alphabetical order. The lab follows this convention unless the target journal or field expects ordering by contribution. The ICMJE recommends that the author group decide the order together, and the lab's teams do so before submission.

## Changes in contribution

Contributions change over a project. A member who leaves early may move from author to acknowledgement. A member who joins late may become an author. The supervisor raises any change with the people affected before it is made and explains the reason.

## AI tools

AI tools are not authors, because they cannot take responsibility for the work. Their use is disclosed as set out in [Document 8](08-ai-use-policy.md).

## Using lab work after you leave

Members may describe their role on a lab project in CVs, applications, and interviews. They may share their own code or writing samples only if these contain no confidential data or unpublished results, or with the supervisor's permission.

## Disagreements

Raise any concern about credit with the supervisor as early as possible. If it cannot be resolved, the department's undergraduate or graduate office can advise.
