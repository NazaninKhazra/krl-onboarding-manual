# 11. Authorship and Credit

Lab projects are team efforts. This policy sets out who becomes a co-author, how other contributions are acknowledged, and how decisions about credit are made. The supervisor discusses it with each team at the start of a project, so that expectations are clear before the work begins.

## Co-authorship

A member is offered co-authorship on a paper when they meet all of the following:

1. They made a substantial contribution to at least one of the following: the research question or design, data collection or construction, the analysis, or the writing.
2. They took part in revising the paper.
3. They approve the final version and can explain and answer for the part of the work they did.

Time spent alone does not qualify a member for authorship. Two weeks of routine data entry, for example, is acknowledged but does not by itself meet the standard. A member who builds the main dataset or writes a section of the paper usually does.

## Acknowledgement

Members who contribute but do not meet the authorship standard are named in the acknowledgements, with a short description of their role (for example, "We thank A. Student for research assistance with data collection").

## Recording contributions

Each paper keeps a contribution record in `docs/contributions.md`, using the CRediT categories (<https://credit.niso.org/>): conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, investigation, methodology, software, visualization, writing (original draft), and writing (review and editing). Members update their own entries, and the supervisor reviews the record at each draft stage.

Weekly reports are the main evidence for this record. Keep them.

## Author order

In economics, authors are usually listed in alphabetical order. The lab follows this convention unless the target journal or field expects ordering by contribution. The team agrees on order before submission.

## Changes in contribution

Contributions change over a project. A member who leaves early may move from author to acknowledgement. A member who joins late may become an author. The supervisor raises any change with the people affected before it is made, and explains the reason.

## Using lab work after you leave

Members may describe their role on a lab project in CVs, applications, and interviews. They may show their own code or writing samples only if these contain no confidential data or unpublished results, or with the supervisor's permission.

## Disagreements

Raise any concern about credit with the supervisor as early as possible. If it cannot be resolved, the department's undergraduate or graduate office can advise.
