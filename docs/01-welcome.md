# 1. Welcome to K.R.L.

The Khazra Research Lab (K.R.L.) for Applied Economics and Data Science is a research group in the Department of Economics at the University of Toronto. Prof. Nazanin Khazra founded the lab in Summer 2025.

Many undergraduates can produce real research by the end of a research-based course such as ECO225. Few of them find a structured way to keep doing it before graduate school. K.R.L. was set up to give these students that opportunity. Its members are undergraduate, MA, and PhD students who work together on applied projects that use real data and aim at publication.

This document explains how the lab is organized and what a new member can expect in the first term.

## The lab's model

At many large universities, an undergraduate joins research as an apprentice to one professor's project. K.R.L. uses a different design. Members form a small research community. They develop projects together under continuous faculty supervision, meet weekly as a group, present their work in public seminars, and share authorship of the papers they produce. Prof. Khazra describes this model in the working paper *Beyond Apprenticeship: A Co-Authored Research Community Model for Undergraduate Economics Education*.

## Roles in the lab

| Role | Terms |
|---|---|
| Student research trainee | An unpaid training position under the University of Toronto's Student Research Trainee letter, with a project description that sets the tasks and deliverable ([Document 2](02-trainee-contract-template.md)). |
| Course-based researcher | A student registered in an independent study (ECO295) or a reading course or thesis (ECO431) whose course work is a lab project. The course outline sets the terms. |
| Paid research assistant | A student hired on a research grant for specific tasks. The hiring letter sets the terms. |
| Graduate research assistant | An MA or PhD student who works on projects that need restricted data or advanced methods, and who also mentors undergraduate members. |

All members attend the same group meetings and seminars, whatever their role.

## How the lab works

Each project has a small team of students and a faculty supervisor. Prof. Khazra proposes the project and its data. The students develop the analysis and write the paper under her supervision.

| Activity | How often | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Full-group meeting | Weekly | Each team reports progress and raises problems |
| Weekly report | Weekly | A short written record of each member's work ([Document 5](05-weekly-report-template.md)) |
| Individual or team meeting with the supervisor | As set in the project description | Research direction and feedback |
| Public seminar | Monthly | Members present work in progress to students, staff, and faculty |

Seminar audiences have ranged from about 30 to over 80 people. Every member is expected to present at least once a year. [Document 12](12-presenting-at-the-seminar.md) explains how to prepare.

The lab uses a Microsoft Teams group with separate channels for each project and for general questions.

## Your first term

The first two weeks are for setup, reading, and a short practice task. By the end of week 2 you should have a signed trainee letter, access to the project folder and repository, and a clear first task. [Document 3](03-first-week-checklist.md) lists each step. A returning member or a graduate assistant will be your first contact for practical questions.

From week 3 onward you work on your project, submit a weekly report, and attend the group meeting. Getting stuck is a normal part of research. Raise problems early in your report or at the group meeting.
