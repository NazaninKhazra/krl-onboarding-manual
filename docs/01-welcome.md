# 1. Welcome to K.R.L.

The Khazra Research Lab (K.R.L.) for Applied Economics and Data Science is a research group in the Department of Economics at the University of Toronto. Prof. Nazanin Khazra founded the lab in Summer 2025. Its members are undergraduate, MA, and PhD students who work as research assistants (RAs) or research trainees on applied projects that use real data and aim at publication.

This document explains how the lab is organized and what a new member can expect in the first term.

## Who is in the lab

Members hold one of the following roles.

- **Research trainees** work on a defined project for a fixed period under a signed trainee agreement (see [Document 2](02-trainee-contract-template.md)). The position is unpaid. Some trainees register for an independent study course instead, where their program allows it.
- **Paid research assistants** are hired on a grant for specific tasks. Their terms are set by the hiring letter.
- **Graduate research assistants** (MA and PhD) work on projects that need restricted data or more advanced methods. They also mentor undergraduate members.

All members attend the same group meetings and seminars, whatever their role.

## How the lab works

Each project has a small team of students and a faculty supervisor. Students develop the project together and share authorship of the output when their contribution meets the standard in [Document 11](11-authorship-and-credit.md).

The lab's regular activities are:

| Activity | How often | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Full-group meeting | Weekly | Each team reports progress and raises problems |
| Weekly report | Weekly | A short written record of each member's work ([Document 5](05-weekly-report-template.md)) |
| Individual or team meeting with the supervisor | As set in the trainee agreement | Research direction and feedback |
| Public seminar | Monthly | Members present work in progress to students, staff, and faculty |

The seminar audience is usually between 30 and 80 people. Every member is expected to present at least once a year. [Document 12](12-presenting-at-the-seminar.md) explains how to prepare.

## Your first term

The first two weeks are for setup and reading. By the end of week 2 you should have access to the project folder and repository, a clear statement of your first task, and a signed trainee agreement. [Document 3](03-first-week-checklist.md) lists each step.

From week 3 onward you work on your project, submit a weekly report, and attend the group meeting. Problems are normal at this stage. Raise them early in your report or at the group meeting.
