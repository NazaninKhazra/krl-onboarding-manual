# 12. Presenting at the Seminar

The lab holds a public seminar each month. Members present work in progress to an audience of students, staff, and faculty, often 30 to 80 people. For many members this is their first research talk. This guide explains how to prepare one.

## Format

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Length | 15 minutes of talk, followed by 5 to 10 minutes of questions |
| Slides | 10 to 12 |
| Speakers | One or two per project. If two, agree in advance who presents which part. |
| Audience | General undergraduate audience. Assume no knowledge of the method. |

## Structure of the talk

1. **Question (1 slide).** State the question in plain language and why someone outside economics should care.
2. **Setting and data (2 slides).** What policy or event you study, where the data come from, and what one observation is.
3. **Method (1 to 2 slides).** The comparison you make and the main assumption. Use a picture of the comparison rather than an equation where you can.
4. **Results (3 to 4 slides).** One main finding per slide. Put the finding in the slide title, for example: "Shelter stays rose after the closure."
5. **Limits and next steps (1 slide).** What the results cannot yet show and what you will do next.
6. **Team and thanks (1 slide).** Name all contributors.

## Slides

- Use large fonts (at least 20 point) and few words per slide.
- Show figures more than tables. If you show a table, highlight the one number you will discuss.
- Label axes and units on every figure.
- Do not show results that the data provider has not cleared for release ([Document 7](07-data-ethics-and-confidentiality.md)).

## Preparation timeline

| When | Step |
|---|---|
| Three weeks before | Agree on the main message with your supervisor. |
| Two weeks before | Send a draft of the slides to your supervisor. |
| One week before | Practice run at the group meeting. Other members give feedback. |
| Two days before | Final slides to the supervisor and the event organizer. |
| On the day | Arrive 20 minutes early to test the projector and your laptop. |

## Answering questions

- Repeat or rephrase the question so the room can hear it.
- If you do not know the answer, say so and say how you might find out. This is an acceptable answer in a research seminar.
- Treat a hard question as a suggestion for the project. Note it down and discuss it with your team afterward.

## After the seminar

Add your slides to the project folder. In your next weekly report, list the questions you received and any that change your plan.
