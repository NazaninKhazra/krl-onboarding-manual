# 7. Data Ethics and Confidentiality

Lab projects use public data, licensed commercial data, and administrative data about people. Each kind carries different rules. Breaking these rules can end a project, cost the lab its access to data, and harm the people the data describe. Read this document before you receive any project data.

## Kinds of data and their rules

| Kind of data | Examples | Main rules |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Census tables, Statistics Canada releases, open city data | Cite the source. Follow the terms of use on the website. |
| Licensed | Retail scanner data, commercial property data | Only people named in the licence may see the data. Do not copy it to personal devices, cloud tools, or AI tools. Results may need review before release. |
| Administrative or confidential | Shelter records, student records, health records | Ethics approval may be required. Store only on approved systems. Report results only at an aggregate level that cannot identify a person. |

Ask your supervisor which category your project's data falls into. If you are not sure, treat the data as confidential.

## Research involving people

Research that uses identifiable information about people usually requires approval from the University of Toronto Research Ethics Board (REB) before work begins. All members who handle such data must complete the TCPS 2: CORE-2022 tutorial (<https://tcps2core.ca/>). The supervisor keeps the certificates on file.

Some secondary data, such as anonymized public-use files, is exempt. The supervisor decides, with the REB where needed, whether approval is required.

## Storage and sharing

- Store project data only in the location your supervisor names. Do not keep copies on USB drives, personal cloud accounts, or shared computers.
- Never upload licensed or confidential data to an AI tool, a website, or a public repository ([Document 8](08-ai-use-policy.md)).
- Do not email data files. Share a link to the approved folder.
- When your appointment ends, delete any local copies and confirm this in your exit checklist ([Document 13](13-closing-packet.md)).

## Reporting results

- Do not report cells with very small counts that could identify a person or a small group. Follow the threshold set by the data provider. If there is none, the lab uses a minimum of 10 observations per cell.
- Do not describe individual cases in a way that someone could recognize, even without a name.
- Some licences require the provider to review results before publication. Check before you present any result outside the lab, including at the lab seminar.

## If something goes wrong

If data is lost, shared by mistake, or seen by someone who should not see it, tell your supervisor the same day. Early reporting allows the lab to limit the damage and meet any reporting duties to the data provider or the University. You will not be penalized for reporting an honest mistake.
