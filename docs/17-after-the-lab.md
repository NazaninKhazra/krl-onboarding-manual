# 17. After the Lab: Presenting, Publishing, and Next Steps

Lab work can lead to conference talks, publications, graduate study, and research jobs. This document lists the main routes open to members and how the lab supports them.

## Presenting beyond the lab

| Venue | Who it is for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| K.R.L. seminar | All members | Monthly. The first place to present work in progress ([Document 12](12-presenting-at-the-seminar.md)). |
| Tech-Econference | Undergraduates | The Department of Economics undergraduate research conference, held each May since 2021. Presenters receive feedback from a faculty panel and take part in a week of presentation workshops beforehand. |
| Masters in Economics Research Symposium (MERS) | MA students | Held each spring since 2026. Each presenter is paired with a PhD-student mentor and gives practice talks before the event. Proceedings: <https://newsletter.economics.utoronto.ca/proceedings-of-the-2026-masters-in-economics-student-research-symposium-mers/> |
| External conferences | Members with advanced work | Discuss with the supervisor before submitting an abstract. Some data licences require results to be cleared first. |

## Publishing

- **Lab papers.** The lab's main outputs are co-authored working papers submitted to journals. Authorship follows [Document 11](11-authorship-and-credit.md).
- **The Invisible Hand.** A student-run undergraduate economics journal at the University of Toronto, which published its first issue in Spring 2026. Prof. Khazra is one of its faculty mentors. It suits individual papers, such as an extended course paper or a literature review.
- **Policy briefs.** Short summaries of lab findings for a general audience ([Document 9](09-writing-guide.md)).

## Graduate school and research jobs

Many members go on to MA or PhD programs, pre-doctoral research positions, or analyst roles in government and industry. To prepare:

- Keep your weekly reports, slides, and code samples. They are the evidence of what you can do.
- Ask your supervisor early, ideally a term before applications open, whether your work is far enough along to support a strong letter.
- Read Jesse Shapiro's notes on applying for a PhD in economics, on his notes and lectures page: <https://shapiro.scholars.harvard.edu/notes-and-lectures>.

## Letters

Every member who completes their deliverable receives a closing letter. Recommendation letters for graduate school and jobs follow the policy in [Document 13](13-closing-packet.md).

## Staying in touch

Former members are listed on the lab's alumni page (<https://krlresearchlab.github.io/alumni.html>) with their consent. Alumni are welcome at the monthly seminar and are often the best people to tell current members about graduate programs and jobs.
